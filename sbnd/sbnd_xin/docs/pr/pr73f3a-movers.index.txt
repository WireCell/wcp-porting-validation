TWO FACTS THAT SET THE SCOPE OF THE SCAN
----------------------------------------
1. The guard never touched an unrelated cosmic.  In all 48 events at least one
   vetoed route lies ON the neutrino candidate's own fitted trajectory
   (T_rec_charge): the largest distance from a vetoed route endpoint to that
   trajectory, over the whole set, is 0.94 cm.  So every change below is a
   change to the neutrino, not collateral damage elsewhere in the event.
   (This is measured geometrically, not by joining the log's cluster ident to
   nusel main_id -- idents are re-enumerated after every visitor, doc 53, and
   that join disagrees with geometry on 7 of these 48.)
2. No event changes WHICH bundle is the neutrino candidate (0/48 candidate
   main_id swaps).  Every dvtx below therefore compares the same object to
   itself; none of them is the artefact of comparing two different bundles.

THE ONE STRUCTURAL QUESTION: SLIDE OR RELOCATE
-----------------------------------------------
For 11 of the 13 events with dvtx > 1 cm, both the old and the new vertex sit
on a trajectory the two arms agree on -- each vertex is at most 2.24 cm from
the OTHER arm's trajectory.  The drawn object is the same; only the chosen
point along it moved.  The scan question there is "which point of this track
is the vertex", not "is this the right object".

Only two are genuine relocations onto different structure:
  57903  (the target; the corridor itself is rebuilt -- that IS the fix)
  30504  (old vertex 3.2 cm off the new trajectory)

51051 is the single ENDPOINT FLIP: the vertex moved from one end of a 301 cm
vetoed route to the other, 247 cm, and the main track re-PIDs pi+ 591 MeV ->
mu 588 MeV with a 108 MeV proton appearing.  That is a track-direction
decision, and it is the one event in the set where the scan verdict is
unambiguous either way -- worth doing first.

pi0: THE PAIRING CHANGES, THE MEASUREMENT MOSTLY DOES NOT
---------------------------------------------------------
kine_pio_mass moves by more than 30 MeV in 15 events that carry the tag in
both arms.  That looks alarming until the pair itself is checked: in 13 of the
15 the two photons being combined are DIFFERENT (E2 or the opening angle moves
by a lot -- 163543 pairs 1057+11 MeV at 7 deg before and 820+113 MeV at 129 deg
after; 30504 goes 244+110 at 138 deg to 33+1009 at 66 deg).  A different pair
is a different quantity, not the same measurement gone worse, so a
toward-135-MeV / away-from-135-MeV tally over those rows means nothing and is
not reported here.

Only TWO events keep the same pair and re-measure it:
  239794  19 -> 51 MeV   (toward 135)
  399860  127 -> 93 MeV  (away from 135)
Two more change the tag itself:
  447477  idx 25  LOSES it (flag 1 -> 0): its second shower splits
                  35.5 -> 25.7 + 39.3 + 33.7 MeV
  55595   idx 45  GAINS it (flag 0 -> 1)

So the scannable pi0 question is "were the right two showers paired", which is
a Bee question, not "did the mass get worse", which the numbers cannot answer.
Full table: f3a_change_map.py --pi0, and columns pio_mass_off / pio_mass_on of
docs/pr/73_f3a_change_map.tsv.
